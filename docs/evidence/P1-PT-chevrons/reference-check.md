# P1-PT-chevrons reference check

Compared the actual kit captures with both named Australian photographs. Side-by-side evidence is in [Helensburgh](frames/reference-helensburgh.jpg) and [Cunninghams Gap](frames/reference-cunninghams-gap.jpg). Sources and licences remain in `art/references/australia/README.md`.

Both show separate, upright yellow boards bearing one black chevron each along the outside of a bend. The post is below/behind the board, and the printed face points toward approaching traffic. The model keeps that arrangement. Left/right bends mirror the printed arrow only; both present the unobstructed front to the approaching driver. The generated-route assertion checks the face normal against travel and the correct arrow direction.

The revised portrait ratio retains the upright single-chevron appearance in both photographs. Absolute size is enlarged for chase-camera/grid readability; these metre values are model choices, not surveyed dimensions from the photographs. The actual 192×108 tile contains 735 yellow and 236 black pixels in the printed-board sample.

With the largest registry post radius (0.10 m), the sheet clears the post by 0.058 m. Two U-bolts sit at ±0.38 m from board centre. Each has an 8 mm rod, a semicircular rear bend, two threaded legs, hex nuts and a backing channel. Bend radius is post radius + rod radius, so its inside touches the post rear at every allowed radius. A saddle spacer touches the post front and board backing channel. Geometry checks cover minimum/default/maximum post radii (40/60/100 mm).

The photos establish silhouette, mounting height relative to the visible post, front orientation and separate boards. They do **not** resolve U-bolt dimensions or rear mounting detail. That detail implements the owner's explicit U-bolt instruction; the side/rear model captures and contact tests verify it, without claiming a photographic measurement.

Reference photos were opened and inspected individually, then both final composites were inspected. The model front, right-arrow, rear and side were also inspected. No multi-chevron board was introduced (R104).

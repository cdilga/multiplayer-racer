# Joystick Jammers — complete experience and visual direction

**Revision:** r12 local WebRTC and efficient controllers; one-screen priority, 30 September 2026.
Companion to the 0.2 revamp plan.
**Direction:** **Outback comic motorsport festival**.
**Product promise:** Put it on the big screen. Grab a controller. Make a spectacular mess.

**Scope priority (R77–R80):** make one big screen excellent: readable Grid/Overview, responsive
WebRTC controllers, clear identity, straightforward joining and a fun complete round. Additional
consuming screens are high effort for low near-term reward. They are a separate project requiring
the owner's explicit G-SCREENS approval before implementation or replication spikes; completing
playtests, extra arenas/modes/themes or Full release does not unlock them. Future screen concepts
in this brief and the UX study are reference material, not active Minimum/Full requirements.

Controller networking uses native WebRTC from the first playable, taking a direct local path where
possible with Cloudflare STUN/TURN initially. Efficient phone/gamepad/keyboard/hub input is required,
not later polish. One QR/code journey works across paths; do not add a networking-mode picker or
ask players to configure TURN. Explain connection/retry state simply; detailed path/latency/usage
evidence belongs in diagnostics and playtest reports. Offline bootstrap is a separate future feature.

This is the design brief for the website, host application, controller, tutorial and game—not a
collection of disconnected mockups. It is proposed creative treatment under the owner's confirmed
comic/Australian/house-party direction. Exact artwork, typography and control mapping are selected
through in-game captures and playtests. No claim is made that the screens already exist.

## 1. The impression in the first five seconds

A beautifully lit, chunky fictional Aussie car bursts through a red-dirt corner. Its suspension is
loaded, one panel has a believable dent, and a brightly patterned rival is close behind. Dust and
inked motion marks communicate speed without covering the road. A clear, confident title sits beside
two unmistakable choices: **HOST A GAME** and **JOIN A GAME**. This looks like a game friends will
laugh at together, not a software dashboard or a gritty military simulator.

The scene must be an actual production-game capture or a lightweight in-engine vignette using the
real assets. A self-hosted compressed still/video fallback gives instant first paint; optional 3D
must not delay the buttons. Never download the world on a controller route just to show branding.
A phone can host, but merely opening the index does not start rendering a game behind a join form.

## 2. A coherent visual system

**Colour and material.** Use warm paper/cream and very dark ink/navy as the readable UI foundation.
Red earth and sun-bleached ochre belong to the Red Centre world; cobalt, saffron and electric teal are
sparingly used festival accents. Player identity colours remain independent and stronger than
optional livery/team trim. Damage adds directional crumpling, scorch and exposed metal, not a brown
overlay that makes every car identical. Emissive elements signal identity, boost, heat and danger
first; decorative glow comes second.

**Shape language.** Cars have bold mechanically legible silhouettes: springy wheel travel, short
readable overhangs and exaggerated distinguishing features. UI shells can lean slightly, with stable
hand-inked edges and layered sticker labels. Text baselines, numerical alignment and touch hit boxes
stay straight. Avoid wobbly body text, thin outline fonts, constantly jittering panels and random
rotations that destroy hierarchy. Comic impact words are a payoff for significant events, not a
per-contact screen covering.

**Typography.** Select and bundle a licensed, bold condensed display face for event titles and a
highly legible sans-serif for controls, labels and body copy. Use stable-width/tabular digits for
scores, positions and countdowns. The exact faces are selected in the art-direction pass, with
locally hosted font files and tested fallback metrics. No remote font service. Names and numbers
must work when longer than the neat mockup examples, including player labels beyond 99.

**Layer hierarchy.** Gameplay/road readability comes first, then personal identity, current action,
objective and immediate threats. Secondary standings, flavour and decorative effects are lower
priority. Separate the world-resolution target from crisp output-resolution text and QR/UI. A quality
change never removes a player or changes collision geometry.

**Motion.** Think energetic motorsport broadcast: an elastic podium reveal, a quick sticker-like
identity entrance, clear countdown beats and a considered replay cut. During driving, camera movement
is useful and predictable. Avoid parallax that fights reading, slow navigation transitions, global
hit-stop and automatic zooms which hide another player. Reduced motion removes excess shake/flashes
without reducing information, changing controls or enabling learner mode.

## 3. Website and room journeys

| Surface | Main job | Specific treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Landing | Choose Host or Join immediately | Real-game hero, two strong CTAs, one sentence explaining shared-screen play; how-to lower on page. No account, installer or release-selector detour. |
| Join | Reach the correct room with confidence | Public room-code field with adjacent **Scan QR code** button (Minimum). Tap to open camera preview; Cancel/Enter code instead retains typed input. Decode locally, stop camera on success/exit/background, and keep manual entry usable when camera access fails. Valid room QR enters the same join flow once; preserve preview/production realm and reject unrelated URLs. No heavy game assets. Clear invalid/expired/reconnecting states. |
| Host setup | Start without a configuration session | Party Mix primary preset, Race/Derby presets visible, advanced details folded away; screen/use profile, sound and capability hints. |
| Lobby | Invite, identify and ready players | Big reliable QR/code, actual player count, identity/car cards, connected/choosing/ready states; no empty numbered slots or max-player selector. |
| Garage | Make a quick personal choice | Strong car silhouette, friendly character sentence and a few meaningful measured stats; Nerd stats behind an expander. Surprise me is explicitly automatic. |
| Tutorial/controller | Drive confidently without looking down | Two enormous stick regions; unmistakable own number/pattern; minimal weapon/damage/status; short matching TV prompts. |
| Host controls | Manage a party without leaving it | Compact pause/settings/identify/source drawer and paired host-control phone; dangerous End/Disband clearly distinct. |
| In-game TV | Follow the action and find yourself | Stable seat-ordered tiles or readable arena Overview, clear personal badges, restrained event calls, no live replays. |
| Intermission | Celebrate and invite the next player | Best actual wrecks/events, readable results/awards, enlarged QR, visible timer, vote on controller; prepare next map out of the way. |
| Event end | Feel like a proper occasion | Podium and inclusive comic awards, night/session summary, rematch or lobby; scores stay meaningful. |
| Preview index | Know exactly what can be tested | Build/scope/evidence/known-gaps/expiry and Play/Back to stable links; no claim that untested lanes passed. |

A device reconnecting must see **Reconnecting as #7** rather than an empty new-player form. A seat
reclaim and an emergency host replacement are different flows, with separate permissions and plain
consequences. When the host confirms its screen is hidden, controllers say **Host paused — back in a moment**;
one controller going away must never show that message to the whole room. An expired preview is not
an ended ordinary room. Unconfirmed host loss says Reconnecting. Errors explain the next useful action without exposing credentials.

Use a coherent component/token library for buttons, cards, badges, panels, toasts, tooltips and
modals. This is an implementation system supporting a distinctive game UI, not permission to use a
generic admin template. Focus styles, screen-reader labels and keyboard/gamepad selection are real
states, not polish postponed until the end.

Minimum shows **Race / automatic rematch**; Full makes Party Mix the default. Garage images are
self-hosted stills/turntable frames, so controller routes never load Three.js or world models.
Code entry works without camera permission; missing optional audio/haptics/storage never blocks
joining. Host capability failure explains how to join or use another host instead of leaving a
nonfunctional room. The implementation contract and 19 journey acceptance rows are in plan §§3.6,
10.12 and 17.5; do not invent different timers, scores or recovery permissions in screen mockups.

The host-control pairing QR is distinct from Join and from the local host's recovery export. Its
phone gets administration, not a seat or permission to replace the host. Sit out/Leave preserve earned
standings and explain what returning does. Recovery import shows the saved cutoff and possible later
loss; stale assignments require host approval. Results distinguish current points from successfully
saved points. End preserves the connected party; Disband revokes it. Finite events stop at EventEnd
with Rematch/Lobby, while the night summary survives an event rematch. Full adds a reviewable Report
form with Retry/Download on failure and a receipt before “Sent”; Minimum offers diagnostic export.

The [eight-screen UX study](ux-study-2026-09-29/README.md) and its
[flow contract](ux-study-2026-09-29/flows.md) make these journeys concrete. Six fresh low-context Luna
readers reviewed first and revised concepts. This is synthetic comprehension evidence, not human
usability validation or final art approval. Generated cars, typography and decorative copy are
illustrative; real production identity tokens must remain consistent across every surface.

Use the revised functional distinctions: **Join the race** claims a seat only after confirmation;
the number appears on acceptance. **Hide replay** preserves voting and the timer; **Start next round
now** advances preparation. **Return everyone to lobby** ends the event, retains connections and
disarms auto-start; **Close room** disconnects everyone. A returning phone shows its identity and
“Waiting for the host”, not another join form. **Ready** can trigger an early start, but is optional
under automatic intermission. If G-SCREENS is later approved, an extra display joins via the same
room QR/code with a display role and is assigned a sensible share automatically, without host
confirmation. Optional manual proportion/player edits show current versus proposed assignment and
require **Apply this layout**. “Manage room from a phone” explicitly keeps simulation on the main
screen; it is administration, not host replacement.

## 4. The two-stick controller and tutorial

Proposed first-playtest mapping: left DRIVE stick handles steering and acceleration/brake/reverse;
right ACTION stick handles forward/back weapon or utility use. Optional horizontal action sectors
provide boost/drift. A deliberate deeper pull on DRIVE visibly preloads for a release wheelie;
ordinary full braking is available before that detent. The mapping can be revised from measured
confusion without reintroducing tap-fire, accelerometer inputs or a screen full of action buttons.

Do not teach every action before first movement. Introduce driving, braking, then front/rear use
against simple training targets using the same core. Wheelie/boost/drift are the next layer. A
matching TV identity and controller cue show where to look and what moved. A tutorial completion
flag is not an admission gate. Late entrants can drive immediately with contextual guidance or use a
brief safe practice presentation; the shared game never pauses for their tutorial.

Show directional arrow/weapon icon, available charge and a readable preload state close to each stick.
No accidental stunt on pointer cancellation, browser suspension or device disconnect. Neutral/re-arm
states must be distinguishable from a broken control. Haptics help where available; all essential
cues are visual. Help can be reopened and uses the person's actual input preset, not touch diagrams
for a keyboard player. Test eyes-down time and accidental actions with newcomers.

**Your controls** offers fixed/floating placement with the same two-stick actions, source response,
haptics and reduced motion. Save preferences on this device across rooms; explain Remember off,
Reset and unavailable storage in contextual help. Opening the menu neutralises input and requests
weak autopilot only for this car; show that status after host acknowledgement. A local input test
pad emits no game actions. Save or Cancel returns through neutral re-arm and deliberate input.
Camera belongs to this player's tile; competitive assists remain separately visible game settings.
The phone has no live world/minimap renderer hidden behind settings. Details and failure fixtures
are plan §10.6a; decorative art must not crowd the thumb controls or make saving ambiguous.

## 5. Worlds designed to drive, not just to decorate

The first theme is a fictional Red Centre motorsport event: ochre dirt, sandstone-like formations,
large sky, dry watercourses and occasional flood crossings. Rock forms frame compelling turns,
gorges and jump approaches. The camera should see the landing and the next decision; a beautiful
blind landing is still a bad beginner route. Keep the existing cultural-respect rules: fictional
landforms, no driving over real sacred monoliths, no cultural motifs used as jokes.

Define route families before prop density: flowing dirt ridge; formed-road sprint into a rough
cut-through; quarry arena; creek-bed jump with a safer crossing; a rock corridor opening into an
overtaking zone. Compose actual curvature, width, elevation, banking and surface transitions.
Barriers protect appropriate corners or mark a deliberate risk; bridges have readable approaches;
ramps have landings compatible with the relevant vehicles. Optional shortcuts trade risk for time.

Venue grammar determines supporting art. A quarry derby can have grandstands, floodlights, marshals'
platforms, tyre walls and a busy pit edge. A remote road stage gets small spectator camps at viewing
points, flags, vehicles and minimal structures rather than continuous stadium seats. Crowds belong
behind the action and support its scale; they must not camouflage the track or identity. Cosmetic
crowd detail can vary with rendering load without changing the driving world.

The six full themes use the approved order: Red Centre, Beach, Outback grassland, Forest, City, Desert
dunes. Each needs a distinct surface/route vocabulary, lighting and skyline, not a different skybox
on the same spline. Reuse feature contracts and materials deliberately. Changes in cosmetic density
must not change seeds' gameplay layout, collision geometry, spawns or checkpoints.

## 6. Vehicles, deformation and readable personality

Every car has a strong one-sentence driving personality, a silhouette readable at reference tile
sizes, and suspension which looks connected to its actual contact state. Identity remains visible
across livery, night lighting, dents, missing doors and dust. Seat number/camera/L-plate anchors are
not attached to a panel which can disappear. Car choice comes from style and distinct tradeoffs,
not an apparently objective ten-bar score with invented numbers.

Author independent crumple regions and damage data for bonnet, doors, bumpers and applicable body
parts. Two separate hits should leave two understandable local deformations. Hinged parts can hang
before detaching; detached geometry retains its dent. Preserve coarse physical collision/clearance
consequences where meaningful without demanding full continuum soft-body simulation before driving
works. The running game—not a Blender beauty render—is final authority on appearance and handling.

## 7. Highlights, sound and copy

Highlights are end-of-round broadcast packages. Keep actual causes: the shove, unstable landing,
wheel leaving the car, the follow-on pileup and the reaction. The director favours multi-player
moments and previously unseen participants, and avoids several near-identical clips. Simple
wide shots are preferable to a cinematic orbit which hides the decisive impact. Do not invent a
better crash by replaying a changed physics world. A short highlight montage can sit within the
roughly one-minute intermission, with results/voting visible rather than making everyone watch a
minute of replay every time.

Offline/build-time FrankenTTS cues and locally generated music/SFX are part of this voice. Captions
make the game work on a muted TV. Australian flavour belongs in optional commentary and awards;
functional copy remains immediate: **Join**, **Ready**, **Brake**, **Reconnecting**. Avoid making every
label an obscure joke. Awards should celebrate funny situations without repeatedly humiliating the
same inexperienced player. Learners can win and winners still get a proper moment.

## 8. Build and review this as one product

Minimum implements the visual foundation and essential landing/join/host/identity/tutorial loop,
using the real Blender-made car. Full finishes every journey, theme, deformation state, audio and
comic rendering. Neither stage has a smaller supported player count. Fixtures are generated from
real state and scalable rosters rather than screenshot-only markup.

Required review surfaces: handheld controller portrait/landscape, phone host, desktop, 1080p TV,
4K TV and unusual aspect ratios; sparse and crowded rooms; long names and labels above 99; damaged
and intact cars; day/dusk/night; loading, paused, disconnected, replaced, expired and ended states.
Use viewing-distance profiles/host override rather than pretending browser pixels reveal physical
TV size or couch distance. QR/readability tests use actual displayed output.

The loop is inspect → build → run → capture stills and motion → compare to the brief → fix the
largest failures → repeat. Verify tile-local post effects, gameplay-visible silhouettes, source
identity and cold-cache self-hosted loading. Owner feedback refines the direction and release gate;
it must not be misreported as approval of unshown screens or untested devices.
